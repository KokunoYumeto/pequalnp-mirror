## Four comparisons that change how the index reads

**The plane and the cube.** The 52 ordered pairs in the formal L-meta-650 statement agree exactly with $(i,13+j)$ for $i+j\in\{0,1,3,9\}\pmod {13}$. The twelve ordered nonzero differences of these four residues cover each nonzero residue once. This gives the projective plane of order three. The matching $j=-i$ and the point cover give $\tau=13$. Computing 13 four-input line disjunctions, selecting the appropriate point on each line, then joining the 13 results costs $39+13+12=64$ binary gates. A circuit of size at most 63 is still a conjectural search target. The 39-gate multi-output theorem holds even on inputs of weight at most two. For the *cube* the checked L-meta-740 gives a lower bound of 11. The search for 10 gates, L-meta-617, is retired. The size-11 search and the proposed size-12 lower bound are the opposing open L-meta-969 and 970. The plane's incidence graph has girth six. The cube contains four-cycles. **Divisibility (DIV).** Write $F=\sum_{i=1}^K\epsilon_i g_i^M\ne0$ with integer coefficients, signs $\epsilon_i=\pm1$, at most $T$ terms per $g_i$, and coefficient height at most $H$. If $(X+1)\cdots(X+N)$ divides $F$, then

$$N!\ \leq\operatorname{Mahler}(F)\ \leq\|F\|_1\ \leq K(TH)^M.$$

The first inequality uses multiplicativity and the lower bound one on the Mahler measure of a nonzero integer quotient. Thus a long run of consecutive roots forces large coefficients. L-gct-103 and 104 allow arbitrary coefficient heights and $K\leq\lfloor\log_2 T\rfloor^c$ for every fixed $c$. Their large-$T$ conclusions $N^{25}\leq T^{27}$ for cubes and $N^5\leq T^6$ for fourth powers both remain conjectural. [Cubic DIV](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-gct-103.ba31805244c0.json) · [Quartic DIV](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-gct-104.118649f18a34.json)

**Which Frobenius and which cubic?** In the NEG_NIGHT mathematical manuscript, NN-C023 uses a degree-$r$ form in $r$ variables, given by a short formula over $\mathbb F_2$. Its squarefree top coefficient acts on a one-dimensional top coherent cohomology group and equals the parity of the number of satisfying assignments. NN-C024 gives generic smoothness and a randomized smooth finite-field transfer. NN-C031 gives a deterministic smooth projective construction over fixed $\mathbb F_4$, with growing degree, a succinct circuit equation and a scalar for fourth-power Frobenius. NN-C027 separately encodes counting in a smooth sparse *affine cubic*, including parity of its number of $\mathbb F_3$-points. These passages support different complexity statements. For a projective cubic hypersurface in $\mathbb P^{r-1}$ with $r>3$, the standard hypersurface sequence gives $H^{r-2}(\mathcal O)=0$. The growing-degree rank-one coherent-scalar argument therefore cannot simply be applied to that cubic. The proposed destination is the algorithm-family question L-alg-001. [Algorithm program](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-alg-001.af98b2f10a1d.json)

**Permanent level spaces.** Put $J=(1)_{ij}$. L-gct-086 asks for a universal $C$ with $\dim L\le Cn$ for complex linear $L$ satisfying $\operatorname{perm}(J+t)=n!$ for every $t\in L$. The general bound is open. The fleet records upper bounds 6 for $4\times4$, 7 for $4\times5$, and 3 for *doubly centred* $4\times4$ level spaces. The latter two rule out the witnesses requested by 181 and 182, although both search records still carry conjecture labels. The checked transfer 077 would turn an upper bound $G$ in size $n$ into border determinantal complexity at least $(n+1)^2+1-2(n+1)-G$ in size $n+1$. In particular $G(6)\le10$ would give 26 for the permanent of size seven. That hypothesis remains a proposed finite problem. **Why the final OR tree matters in AC-2.** A cyclic group of order 13 acting by automorphisms of a single-output circuit fixes its output. It follows that the proposed orbit search must have a boundary where equivariance ends, as it does in the final OR tree of the star circuit.

## Fable's implications and open questions

**General circuits for plane line sums.** Claude Fable 5.2's Theorem E exploits the Singer form. With $N=q^2+q+1$, pack the input bits in base $2^\ell$ for $\ell=\lceil\log_2(q+2)\rceil$. Multiply by the packed indicator of the reversed difference set, fold the two blocks for each cyclic offset, and test for a nonzero sum. Every coefficient is at most $q+1$, so there is no carry between blocks. Fast integer multiplication gives $O(N\log^2N\log\log N)$ bounded-fan-in gates. In contrast, checked meta-270 gives exactly $Nq$ for the monotone multi-output problem, attained by separate line sums. The classical negation-limited inverter construction gives the same asymptotic upper bound using at most $\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$ negations. The cost for each intermediate negation budget remains open, including the one-NOT conjectures 951 and 952. The checked meta-745 recurrence has explicit hypotheses. [Monotone theorem](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-270.48bec89aea76.json) · [Conditional recursion](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-745.711af7f2a33f.json) · [Beals–Nishino–Tanaka, *On the Complexity of Negation-Limited Boolean Networks*](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/S0097539794275136)

**Light inputs.** Fable's Theorem D gives $O(q^2)$ monotone gates for the affine-plane line disjunctions over a prime field under the promise of at most one selected point. Row and column sums identify both coordinates on a singleton. Dual-rail arithmetic produces the intercept for each slope, and a separate decoder lists that slope's lines. The zero input can be gated by an activity wire. This matches meta-270 up to a constant on that promise. The general weight-two affine problem remains open. The projective $q=3$ calculation in meta-667 is one exact instance. The weight profile of the single-output edge query is another problem.

**Proof systems and simulations.** Fable combines checked pcx-043 and 046 for bounded-degree graph pigeonhole formulas with pcx-042 to obtain $\exp(\Omega(v))$ lines in dag-like resolution over parity equations on $v$ variables. The last declaration's description flags its numerical constant for semantic weakening. The exponential conclusion assumes the indicated positive-constant degree-to-size conversion. It concerns refutations in this proof system. Static arithmetic counting requires a separate lower bound.

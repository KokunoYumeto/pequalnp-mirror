# Twelve level-six markings and the trace of the Monster 6B function

On the elliptic curve $E:y^2=x^3-3x+1$ over $\mathbb Q$, the twelve cyclic subgroups of order six form one Galois orbit. Each marking gives a value of the normalized Monster $6B$ function of degree twelve. The sum of these values is $1500$, so their arithmetic mean is $125$. More generally, the sum over all markings on the generic elliptic curve is $j-804$, with $j=q^{-1}+744+\cdots$.

## The two modular functions

The choice of the level-six coordinate and its Fricke combination follows The Clankers, *Support-prime moonshine completion of the residual modulo-840 Erdős–Straus datum* (May 2026), in the section on the Fricke involution. The field trace below uses the map that forgets the cyclic subgroup of order six.

Put $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$ and $\eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)$. The coordinate on $X_0(6)$ used here is

$$
x=\frac{\eta(\tau)^5\eta(3\tau)}{\eta(2\tau)\eta(6\tau)^5}.
$$

Maier's Table 2 gives the coordinate $t_6=72/x$, and his Table 4 gives the rational parametrization of the ordinary $j$-invariant. In the coordinate $x$ the map is

$$
j=\frac{A(x)}{B(x)},\quad
A(X)=(X+12)^3(X^3+252X^2+3888X+15552)^3,\quad
B(X)=X^6(X+8)^2(X+9)^3 .
$$

The two polynomials are coprime. The map has degree twelve and $x$ has a single simple pole, at the infinite cusp. The arithmetic specialization below uses the member $t=4/3$ of the elliptic family with $t=j/1728$. The Fricke involution $W_6:\tau\mapsto-1/(6\tau)$ reverses the four eta exponents under $d\mapsto6/d$. They therefore change sign. The eta transformation contributes the scalar $6^{5/2}3^{-1/2}2^{1/2}=72$, so $x(W_6\tau)=72/x(\tau)$. The invariant field is generated by $x+72/x$. From the product expansion,

$$
x=q^{-1}-5+6q+4q^2+O(q^3),\qquad
M=x+5+72/x=q^{-1}+78q+364q^2+1365q^3+O(q^4).
$$

The additive $5$ makes the constant coefficient zero. The normalized generator for $\langle\Gamma_0(6),W_6\rangle$ is thus $M$. Conway and Norton assign their group symbol $6+6$ to Monster class $6B$ in Table 2 (p. 327). Their Table 4 (p. 334) gives the positive-degree coefficients $78,364,1365$ in that column. Their value $7$ in the auxiliary degree-zero head-character row belongs to a separate choice of constant. Their uppercase normalized Hauptmodul has zero constant, as specified in §2. Borcherds' theorem identifies that normalized series with the graded Monster trace $\sum_{n\ge-1}\operatorname{tr}(g\mid V^\natural_{n+1})q^n$, using conformal-weight grading and $g\in6B$. ## A field trace from Vieta's formulas

In the degree-twelve extension $\mathbb Q(x)/\mathbb Q(j)$, the twelve conjugates $x_i$ are the roots of the monic polynomial $P(X)=A(X)-jB(X)$. The coefficient of $X^{11}$ in $P$ is $3\cdot12+3\cdot252-j=792-j$. At the other end, $P(0)=A(0)\ne0$, and

$$
\frac{P'(0)}{P(0)}=\frac{3}{12}+
3\frac{3888}{15552}=1 .
$$

The first expression gives $\sum_i x_i=j-792$. Logarithmically differentiating $P(X)=\prod_i(X-x_i)$ at zero gives $\sum_i1/x_i=-1$. Consequently the field trace of the scalar modular function is

$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q(x)/\mathbb Q(j)} M
=(j-792)+12\cdot5-72=j-804 .
$$

## An irreducible arithmetic fibre

The discriminant of $E$ is $1296$, and $j(E)=2304$. Over $\mathbb F_5$ the cubic takes the values $1,4,3,4,3$ at the five abscissae in order. Hence $E$ has seven points including infinity. Frobenius on the prime-to-five torsion has characteristic polynomial $Z^2+Z+5$.

An order-six marking consists of a line in $E[2]$ and a line in $E[3]$. Modulo 2 this characteristic polynomial is irreducible, giving a three-cycle on the three lines. Modulo 3 it is again irreducible. Frobenius acts there by a nonscalar element of $\mathbb F_9^\times$, so its projective order divides four. Cayley–Hamilton and its nonzero trace imply its square is nonscalar. Its projective order is four, giving a four-cycle on the four lines. The product of cycles of coprime lengths three and four is a twelve-cycle on the markings.

Since $j(E)\ne0,1728$, its geometric automorphisms $\{\pm1\}$ fix each subgroup. The marked points are therefore distinct on $X_0(6)$, and every $x_i$ has degree twelve over $\mathbb Q$. To establish the same degree for $M_i$, one must also exclude a pair $x_i x_k=72$. Write $P_*=A-2304B$. This polynomial is irreducible by transitivity. If two roots had product $72$, then $X^{12}P_*(72/X)$ would be proportional to $P_*$. Reduce this putative identity modulo 5. The constant, linear and eleventh-degree coefficients of the monic $P_*$ reduce respectively to $-1,-1,-2$. The leading coefficient of the reciprocal polynomial is therefore $-1$, so the proportionality constant reduces to $-1$. Its eleventh-degree coefficient reduces to $72(-1)=-2\bmod5$; the corresponding coefficient of $-P_*$ is $2$. This is a contradiction.

The identity $M(x)-M(y)=(x-y)(1-72/(xy))$ now gives twelve distinct conjugate values. Substitution into the generic trace yields $2304-804=1500$. The corresponding sum of the twelve original $x_i$ is $1512$. Both sums are rational even though each individual value generates a degree-twelve field.

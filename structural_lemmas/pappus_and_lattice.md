# Two incidence planes and the marked Leech lattice

The ternary plane supplies an exact identification of two circuit problems. A second plane, over the four-element field, describes how two involution actions fit into a single group on the Leech lattice. The two constructions have different point sets, coefficients and circuit consequences.

## Ternary glue, elliptic flexes and the Pappus graph

**Tetracode incidence.** Write the ternary tetracode as the nine vectors

$$
   (a+b,a-b,a,b),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb F_3 .
$$

For each coordinate and each value, mark its fibre. The four functionals have distinct kernels and each fibre has three elements. Thus the vectors are the points of the affine plane of order three, and the twelve fibres are exactly its lines. This is the tetracode that occurs in the ternary glue of the Niemeier lattice of root type $A_5^4D_4$. Its umbral lambency is 6. This identification uses the glue code; no Fourier coefficient of an umbral form is needed. [Cheng, Duncan and Harvey, *Umbral Moonshine and the Niemeier Lattices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5793)

**The 18-vertex graph.** Omit all three fibres of the fourth coordinate. The retained incidence graph has nine points, nine lines and twenty-seven edges. It is the Pappus graph. To compare with the exact fleet statement, enumerate the points in lexicographic order of $(a,b)$; their fleet labels are 0,16,12,14,6,8,10,2,4. Enumerate the retained lines by coordinate index then value; their labels are 1,15,11,5,13,9,17,7,3. Testing coordinate equality gives exactly the 27 fleet edges. The incidence matrix in these orders has integral Smith entries $1,1,1,1,1,1,3,0,0$, hence ranks seven over the rationals and over the binary field, and six over the ternary field. [Pappus configuration](https://aeb.win.tue.nl/graphs/Pappus.html) · [L-meta-458](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-458.3fb29b1527a1.json) · [Reproducible check](checks/pappus_marking.py)

The nine codewords index separate point wires. All $2^9$ Boolean inputs are admissible for the nine line outputs, and all $2^{18}$ point-and-selector inputs are admissible for the single edge output. The same line system underlies the fleet's checked L-meta-907. Its nine point-to-line disjunctions jointly require eighteen binary gates even if one NOT gate is free; two OR gates per output attain eighteen. L-meta-458 instead supplies a separate selector bit for each line and asks for **one** output, allowing arbitrary binary OR gates at no cost and at most eight binary AND gates. Nine AND gates suffice by forming the line disjunctions and multiplying each by its selector. An edge-cover by complete bipartite subgraphs requires at least nine stars since this graph has no four-cycle and has a perfect matching. This latter argument treats the star construction. Arbitrary monotone circuits can have deeper gates and reuse intermediate values. A proof handling this reuse or a verified eight-AND circuit would settle the proposed target. [L-meta-907 proof](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/pull/1494) · [Fleet search statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-458.3fb29b1527a1.json)

**Where the elliptic invariant enters.** On a smooth complex plane cubic, choose a flex as origin. The nine flexes are $E[3]$. Three distinct ones are collinear exactly when their sum is zero, so the twelve triples are the affine lines above. The additional datum for the Pappus construction is a cyclic subgroup of order three, whose parallel class is omitted. For the Gaussian curve with $j=1728$, multiplication by $i$ on $E[3]$ has characteristic polynomial $X^2+1$, irreducible over $\mathbb F_3$. It pairs up the four choices in two transpositions. For the Eisenstein curve with $j=0$, multiplication by a primitive cube root has one eigenline and cycles the other three. Indeed on the Eisenstein integral basis its matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; modulo three this has one Jordan block with eigenvalue one. The resulting marked graphs are isomorphic as abstract graphs. What the observation distinguishes is the action of the distinguished complex multiplication on the markings. [Artebani and Dolgachev, *The Hesse Pencil of Plane Cubic Curves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0611590) · [Milne, *Elliptic Curves*](https://jmilne.org/math/Books/EC2.pdf)

In the displayed basis the Eisenstein-invariant subgroup has equation $a+b=0$. The coordinate dictionary omits the kernel of $b$. To use the invariant marking, omit the first coordinate instead, or make the corresponding change of ternary basis. This distinction matters when transporting an automorphism to the labelled circuit.

## The two traces on twenty-four coordinates

**Simultaneous lattice action.** Use the extended quadratic-residue Golay model of the Leech lattice on $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{23})$, with infinity numbered 23. Use fractional-linear maps

$$
  C=\begin{pmatrix}12&5\\1&12\end{pmatrix},\quad
  J=\begin{pmatrix}1&7\\3&22\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Let $B=\{0,1,3,4,7,8,16,23\}$. Its three translates under $C$ are disjoint octads. Let $P$ be the Klein four-group generated by the conjugates of $J$ by $C$, and $S$ the Klein four-group that negates two octads at a time. In the three-octonion identification a two-block sign change is precisely an involution of the marked Albert frame on its off-diagonal space. The cyclic Golay permutation used here cycles the octads; on the individual octonion coordinates it has an additional action. Wilson's cyclic slot permutation is a separate operator. [Wilson's octonionic Leech construction](https://webspace.maths.qmul.ac.uk/r.a.wilson/pubs_files/octoLeech1rev.pdf)

Each element of $P$ fixes the octads setwise, so the two four-groups commute and intersect trivially. Conjugation by $C$ cycles the three nonidentity elements of each. Consequently their group with $C$ is

$$
       H=(S\times P)\rtimes\langle C\rangle \simeq \mathbb F_4^2\rtimes\mathbb F_4^\times .
$$

To obtain the field structure on each two-dimensional binary summand, let the primitive element of $\mathbb F_4$ act by conjugation by $C$. Its minimal polynomial is $X^2+X+1$. On the five one-dimensional $\mathbb F_4$ subspaces of the translation subgroup, there is one distinguished direction. Its three nonzero elements have trace −8: each fixes eight coordinates and negates sixteen. All twelve nonzero translations on the other four directions have trace zero, since their permutation component has no fixed coordinate. The full group preserves the integral lattice. The checker builds an integral basis from its Golay numerator generators and checks the three generators on that basis. [Coordinate and group calculation](checks/joint_action.py)

The marked off-diagonal Albert cubic is $2\operatorname{Re}((z_0z_1)z_2)$. In the octad-aligned frame specified in the checker, it has 64 nonzero monomials. Acting on each coefficient with all 48 signed permutations gives stabilizer exactly $S$ and twelve different cubics. None is the negative of the starting cubic. These are finite coefficient checks for this specified frame. They identify exactly which part of the group preserves the cubic while the whole group preserves the lattice. Reproducibility requires the octonion signs and the signed identification of the octads, both supplied in the program.

**The projective-plane realization.** The same abstract group acts faithfully on $\mathrm{PG}(2,4)$ by $[x:y:z]\mapsto[tx+az:ty+bz:z]$. On its 21 points the orbits have sizes $16,1,1,1,1,1$. On its 21 lines they have sizes $1,4,4,4,4,4$. The ordinary characters agree: the numbers fixed are 21 for the identity, 5 for a nonzero translation and 6 for a remaining homothety. The numbers fixed by the **entire** group are five and one. In characteristic two the distinguished permutation dot products on the simultaneous invariant spaces have ranks five and one. Indeed the orbit sums form a basis of the invariants, and their Gram entries on the diagonal are the orbit sizes. This gives a calculable consequence of the relationship to the 24-dimensional action. Each plane action is a separate representation of the same group.

**Why the number eighteen requires a marking.** Write $T$ for the three-dimensional rational tetrahedral representation of $A_4$, and $U$ for the inflated regular representation of its cyclic quotient of order three. The Albert slot action is $8T$, and the Golay permutation action is $2U+6T$, since the 24 coordinates form two regular orbits. Their largest common rational summand is $6T$, of dimension eighteen. A permutation module containing $6T$ requires at least **24 points**. For a transitive orbit $A_4/K$ the multiplicity of $T$ equals $\dim T^K$. For a stabilizer of type $1,C_2,C_3,V_4,A_4$ the orbit sizes are $12,6,4,3,1$ and the respective multiplicities are $3,1,1,0,0$. Each copy of $T$ thus costs at least four points; two regular orbits attain 24. A direct identification with the Pappus vertex permutation module is thereby excluded. The explicit ternary-incidence map above supplies a different, testable route to that graph.

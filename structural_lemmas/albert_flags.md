# Fano flags from three Leech reflections, and ternary Golay planes modulo nine

Three reflections in the octonionic Leech construction generate 21 rank-one projectors. Their orthogonal triples recover the seven points and seven lines of the Fano plane. A Klein four acting on these two sets gives a pair of six-sheet actions with the same permutation character and different fixed-point counts. In the ternary construction of the Leech lattice, exactly **1,320** two-dimensional subspaces of the extended ternary Golay code admit lifts modulo nine on which both the quadratic and fourth moments vanish.

## The 21 projectors

Use the quadratic field $E=\mathbb Q(s)$, where $s^2+s+2=0$ and $\bar s=-1-s$. For a nonzero row $v\in E^3$, write $P_v=v^*v/(vv^*)$. This is a trace-one idempotent in the Hermitian $3\times3$ matrices, with $P_{cv}=P_v$ for $c\ne0$. In Wilson's octonionic coordinates one takes $s=(-1+i_0+\cdots+i_6)/2$. The rows

$$
r_1=(2,2,0),\qquad r_2=(2s,0,0),\qquad r_3=(s^2,s,s)
$$

are shortest Leech vectors in Wilson's normalization and are the first three rows of Nasmith's six-vector construction. His reflection in $r$ acts on rows by $v\mapsto v(I-2P_r)$. All three matrices here have entries in the same associative field $E$, so ordinary matrix multiplication suffices. Nasmith's six reflections generate an orbit of 819 projectors in the octonionic projective plane. His Example 5.2 also identifies a projective $\mathrm{PSL}_2(7)$ for the signed coordinate permutations of $r_3$. [Robert A. Wilson, *Octonions and the Leech lattice*, §§2–3](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/wilsonleech.pdf) · [Benjamin Nasmith, *Octonions and the two strictly projective tight 5-designs*, Definition 4.1, Theorem 4.4 and Example 5.2](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/alco.215.pdf)

**Flag coordinates.** Write $E_i$ for the ray of $e_i$, $B_{ij}^{\pm}$ for that of $e_i\pm e_j$, and $C_i^{ab}$ for the ray having $s$ in position $i$ and signs $a,b$ in the other positions, in increasing coordinate order. In the table, encode vectors of $\mathbb F_2^3$ by $1,\ldots,7$ with basis $1,2,4$. Rows are nonzero points $p$, columns are nonzero line normals $n$, and a cell is occupied precisely when $n^\mathsf{T}p=0$.

| $p$ / $n$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | | $B_{12}^{-}$ | | $E_3$ | | $B_{12}^{+}$ | |
| 2 | $B_{23}^{-}$ | | | $E_1$ | $B_{23}^{+}$ | | |
| 3 | | | $B_{13}^{-}$ | $E_2$ | | | $B_{13}^{+}$ |
| 4 | $C_1^{++}$ | $C_3^{++}$ | $C_2^{++}$ | | | | |
| 5 | | $C_3^{--}$ | | | $C_1^{+-}$ | | $C_2^{+-}$ |
| 6 | $C_1^{--}$ | | | | | $C_3^{-+}$ | $C_2^{-+}$ |
| 7 | | | $C_2^{--}$ | | $C_1^{-+}$ | $C_3^{+-}$ | |

The three seed cells are $(1,6),(2,4),(4,1)$. For each such $(u,f)$, the reflection acts on the table by

$$
(p,n)\longmapsto(gp,g^{-\mathsf T}n),\qquad g=I+uf^\mathsf T.
$$

This gives a 63-image certificate using the table and the single substitution $v\mapsto v-2(vr^*)r/(rr^*)$. For example, reflecting $B_{12}^{+}$ in $C_1^{++}$ gives a row proportional to $(1,s,-1)$, the entry $C_2^{+-}$ at $(5,7)$. The three binary matrices are $I+E_{12}+E_{13}, I+E_{23}, I+E_{31}$, where $E_{ij}$ denotes a matrix unit. The commutator of the first two is $I+E_{13}$. Multiplying by the first gives $I+E_{12}$. Commutators of the three cyclic elementary transvections give the remaining elementary transvections, so the matrices generate $\mathrm{GL}(3,2)$. Its transitivity on incident point-line pairs proves that the three seed reflections generate all 21 rays in the table.

For two rays the Albert trace product is

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(P_v\circ P_w)=\frac{(vw^*)\overline{(vw^*)}}{(vv^*)(ww^*)},
\qquad A\circ B=(AB+BA)/2 .
$$

For the seed $E_1$, the trace against $P_w$ is $|w_1|^2/(ww^*)$. It is zero on the other four cells of its row and column, $1/4$ on eight cells and $1/2$ on eight. Transitivity gives the same distribution at every cell. Thus two distinct projectors are orthogonal exactly when they share a row or a column. The 14 mutually orthogonal triples are precisely the rows and columns. Their intersections recover Fano incidence from the trace alone. Taking the rays as points and the triples as lines subdivides each edge of the Fano incidence graph once. The resulting incidence graph has girth 12 and diameter 6, and hence gives a generalized hexagon of order $(2,1)$.

## Which trace distinguishes the six sheets?

Let $D=\{\operatorname{diag}(a,b,ab):a,b=\pm1\}$. Projectively it is generated by coordinate-axis reflections. The three axes occur in column 4, so on Fano points its four matrices are $I+u e_3^\mathsf T$ with $u\in\langle e_1,e_2\rangle$. They fix points $1,2,3$ and act regularly on $4,5,6,7$. On line normals they fix 4 and have pairs $\{1,5\},\{2,6\},\{3,7\}$. Deleting one fixed point and the unique fixed line gives $D$-sets $X,Y$ with orbit sizes $(4,1,1)$ and $(2,2,2)$. Every involution fixes two sheets on either side. All of $D$ together fixes two sheets of $X$ and zero of $Y$.

The same $D$-sets occur among the rays themselves. For $X$ take the four $C_1^{ab}$ and axes $E_1,E_2$. For $Y$ take the six $B_{ij}^{\pm}$. The trace of coordinatewise multiplication of sheet functions is the ordinary dot product. On the orbit indicators its Gram determinants are $4\cdot1\cdot1=4$ and $2^3=8$, in different square classes modulo 13. For the Albert trace, the orbit sums on $X$ are $\operatorname{diag}(2,1,1),E_{11},E_{22}$, while those on $Y$ are the three matrices $E_{ii}+E_{jj}$. Their Gram determinants are 1 and 4, both squares. In fact the two triples span the same diagonal space. Thus replacing the distinguished sheet trace by this restricted Albert trace loses the discriminant test.

The same coordinates give a seven-sheet graph example. Take the reflection words $12,121232,2$, with the rightmost reflection acting first. Their binary point matrices have columns $(1,3,6),(4,2,7),(1,2,6)$. For each of the 128 point subsets $S$ put $w(S)=\sum_g|S\mathbin{\triangle}gS|$, summing over the three words, and form the analogous line histogram. The point-minus-line histogram polynomial is

$$
2z^2-10z^4-4z^6+28z^8-6z^{10}-18z^{12}+8z^{14}.
$$

Use each word as the closing edge transport of one triangle in a three-triangle bouquet. At cut activity two the equal-end and unequal-end path weights are 13 and 14. A histogram term at degree $w$ therefore acquires weight $13^{21-w}14^w$. The term of degree 14 gives the first nonzero digit of the cut difference, $8\bmod13$ after division by $13^7$. ## Which ternary planes lift modulo nine?

Let $W\le\mathbb F_3^{12}$. A lift here means an additive subgroup $U\le(\mathbb Z/9)^{12}$ reducing onto $W$ such that both $\sum_i t_i^2$ and $\sum_i t_i^4$ vanish modulo nine for every $t\in U$.

**Length-twelve criterion.** Such a lift exists exactly when every nonzero word of $W$ has Hamming weight nine. Necessarily $\dim W\le2$. At dimension two, signs and a coordinate permutation put the coordinate forms in four groups of three identical copies of $x,y,x+y,x-y$.

Indeed $2t^4-t^2\equiv\mathbf1_{3\nmid t}\pmod9$. For a unit put $t^2=1+3a$, and for a nonunit both powers vanish. Summing gives the weight condition. If $r=\dim W>0$ and $n'$ coordinates are active, counting nonzero coordinate evaluations gives

$$
9(3^r-1)=2\cdot3^{r-1}n'.
$$

At $r=3$ this requires 13 active coordinates, with still more required at higher rank. For $r=2$ all twelve coordinates are active. Exactly three vanish at each projective input, so each of the four dual directions occurs three times. Conversely, use the four displayed integer forms in triplicate. Their squares and fourth powers each sum to zero modulo three on the four directions, since a nonzero input makes precisely three forms nonzero. Triplication gives zero modulo nine for all lifts of the inputs. Restore the coordinate signs and permutation. For a line, scalar multiples of its weight-nine generator with entries $0,\pm1$ already satisfy both equations. The zero space has the zero lift. This instance of the repeated-simplex description also follows from the classification of constant-weight linear codes. [Arrigo Bonisoli, *Every equidistant linear code is a sequence of dual Hamming codes*](https://unimore.unifind.cineca.it/resource/item/19917?language=en-US)

**Exactly 1,320 planes in the extended ternary Golay code.** Let $C$ be the self-dual ternary $[12,6,6]$ code. Its nonzero weights are multiples of three by self-orthogonality. Projection onto any two coordinates is surjective because the dual distance is six. Write $A_j$ for the number of words of weight $j$. Counting words, zero positions and unordered pairs of zero positions among nonzero words gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_6+A_9+A_{12}&=728,\\
6A_6+3A_9&=12(3^5-1),\\
15A_6+3A_9&=\binom{12}{2}(3^4-1).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $(A_6,A_9,A_{12})=(264,440,24)$. Up to sign there are 220 weight-nine words and 12 full-weight words. Each weight-nine projective word is determined by its triple of zeros. Indeed, if two independent words $u,v$ had the same zero triple, the supports of $u+v$ and $u-v$ would partition the other nine coordinates. Both have weight at least six, which is impossible. Since $\binom{12}{3}=220$, every zero triple occurs. [Robert L. Griess Jr., *Pieces of Eight*, construction (4.8) and Theorem 4.10](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/griess2.pdf)

Fix one weight-nine projective word $u$. In an eligible plane the four projective words have disjoint zero triples. This leaves $\binom93=84$ candidates $v$ for a second word. Since their zero triples are disjoint, the weights of $u+v,u-v$ add to 18. Both are multiples of three and at least six, so the two weights are either $9,9$ or $6,12$ in some order.

Each of the 12 full-weight projective words $z$ accounts for exactly one unsuccessful candidate. Indeed the weights of $z+u,z-u$ add to 15, so they are $6,9$ in some order. The weight-nine one has zero triple disjoint from that of $u$. Conversely an unsuccessful pair $u,v$ has exactly one full-weight projective word in its plane. Thus $84-12=72$ choices succeed. Each plane through $u$ uses three of them. There are **24 planes through each weight-nine line**, hence $220\cdot24/4=\mathbf{1,320}$ planes.

In the Niemeier lattice with root system $A_2^{12}$, this code is the quotient by the root lattice. The inverse image of each eligible plane has index nine over $A_2^{12}$ and determinant $3^{12}/9^2=3^8$. In Griess's ternary construction of the rootless Leech lattice, the same code indexes commuting rotations through $0,\pm120^\circ$ on twelve real coordinate planes. A codeword of weight $w$ acts with trace $2(12-w)-w=24-3w$. Consequently the eligible planes are exactly the order-nine subgroups of this specified code group whose 24-dimensional character equals three times the augmentation character. Its values are 24 at the identity and $-3$ at every other element. Each nonidentity rotation then fixes six real dimensions, while the whole subgroup has zero fixed space. [Robert L. Griess Jr. and Ching Hung Lam, *Diagonal lattices and rootless $EE_8$ pairs*, Appendix A.2](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/rlg/wp-content/uploads/sites/1335/2024/09/sdc-11jan2011.pdf) · [Griess, construction (4.8)](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/griess2.pdf)

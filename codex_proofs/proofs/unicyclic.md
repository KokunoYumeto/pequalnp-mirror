# Optimal monotone circuits for graphs with one cycle of length at least six

Let \(G\) be a finite connected simple graph whose unique cycle has length at least six. Its edge function takes one Boolean input per vertex and accepts precisely those inputs selecting the endpoints of at least one edge. Write \(m=|E(G)|\) and \(t=\tau(G)\), the minimum vertex-cover size.

**Theorem.** The minimum number of gates in a circuit of fan-in at most two over AND and OR computing the edge function, with unrestricted fanout, is \(m+t-1\).

For the upper bound, take a minimum vertex cover and assign every edge to one of its endpoints in the cover. Each cover vertex owns at least one edge, since it has a neighbor outside the cover by minimality. Its star with \(d\) owned edges uses \(d-1\) OR gates and one AND. Joining the stars takes \(t-1\) more gates. This is the vertex-cover construction for quadratic Boolean functions. [Jukna, §1](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/ftp/single1.pdf)

For the lower bound, consider a leaf \(u\) with neighbor \(v\). In any positive circuit for the edge function the input \(u\) is essential, hence has a path to the output. An OR-only path would accept the singleton \(u\). At the first AND on such a path, setting \(u=1\) makes its path operand true. Fix also \(v=0\). Bypassing that AND at all its consumers and propagating constants produces a circuit for \(G-\{u,v\}\) using at least one fewer AND. Free auxiliary constants only strengthen this lower-bound argument. Replacing \(u\) by \(v\) in a minimum cover if necessary gives \(\tau(G)=1+\tau(G-\{u,v\})\).

Choose leaves successively at maximum distance from the unique cycle, discarding isolated vertices after each step. If a step deletes a cycle vertex, a forest remains. Continuing the leaf reductions to exhaustion shows that the original circuit contains at least \(t\) ANDs. Every induced subgraph of \(G\) is a pseudoforest, so spans at most as many edges as vertices. Also \(G\) has no four-cycle. Under these hypotheses the OR lower bound L-meta-599 gives at least \(m-1\) ORs in the original circuit. Adding the two bounds gives the result in this case. [L-meta-599, proof for four-cycle-free pseudoforests](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/554253c04c90db722fc689346a6947081d8c4dbc/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_599.lean)

Otherwise the bare cycle survives. Each deleted off-cycle parent \(v\) had a surviving neighbor \(r\) and \(d\ge1\) leaf children. Reinstating this star adds \(d+1\) edges and increases the minimum cover by one. Let \(H\) be the surviving graph at that step. In an ordinary circuit over AND, OR and NOT for the enlarged graph, set \(d-1\) of the fresh leaves to zero. Each is essential at its deletion, witnessed by \(v\) alone, and the restricted function remains nonconstant because the cycle survives. Essential-input restriction saves at least one gate each time. The remaining function has the form \(f_H\lor (x_v\land(x_r\lor x_u))\) with \(v,u\) fresh. The two-fresh-input lower bound L-meta-547 costs three gates above the ordinary minimum for \(f_H\). Its hypotheses hold because an edge gives a true input, while the empty set and the singleton \(r\) give false inputs on the two slices of \(x_r\). The cost therefore rises by at least \(d+2\), the precise increment of \(m+t-1\). [Two-fresh-input lower bound](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/554253c04c90db722fc689346a6947081d8c4dbc/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_547.lean) · [Essential-input restriction, L-meta-014](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-014.17bbe9d3c481.json)

On the bare cycle there are six consecutive degree-two vertices. The unrestricted circuit lower bound L-meta-520 applies. Restoring all the stars proves the ordinary lower bound in the surviving-cycle case, and hence its monotone lower bound. The earlier case uses positivity at the AND-elimination step. [L-meta-520](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-520.8ca33af959dd.json)

For example, a six-cycle with a single leaf at each alternate vertex has nine edges and minimum cover size three. Its positive optimum is eleven gates. The three marked vertices cover every edge, and the three pendant edges form a matching.

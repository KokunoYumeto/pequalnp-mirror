# Counting unstable line outputs from the size of a Boolean circuit

Let a circuit with shared fan-in-two AND and OR gates and unary NOT gates compute the line disjunctions \(f_i(x)=\bigvee_{v\in S_i}x_v\) on all Boolean assignments. Distinct \(S_i\) meet in at most one point. Constants and projections can be simplified. Let \(B\) count binary AND and OR gates. For each line, set its own inputs to zero and all inputs outside the line to the unknown value \(u\). Evaluate the original gates in strong Kleene logic (\(0<u<1\), AND is minimum, OR maximum, and NOT fixes \(u\)). Every Boolean completion has output zero on this line. Say the output is *stable on its test* when the three-valued circuit also outputs zero.

**Proposition.** If \(J\) is the set of lines stable on their tests, then

\[
\sum_{i\in J}\max\{|S_i|-1,0\}\ \le B.
\]

**Proof.** Compute the Boolean baseline at the all-zero input on every wire. For any set \(T\) of positions, let \(D_w(T)\) record whether wire \(w\) becomes unknown when precisely \(T\) is set to \(u\), keeping the other inputs zero. The output of each wire is either its baseline or unknown. For an AND gate with operand baselines \(00,01,10,11\), its unknown indicator is respectively \(d_1\land d_2,d_1,d_2,d_1\lor d_2\). For OR in the same order the four expressions are \(d_1\lor d_2,d_2,d_1,d_1\land d_2\). A negation copies its operand's unknown indicator. This produces a single shared positive circuit for all \(D_w\) at a cost of at most one gate per original binary gate. Baseline-constant terminals have zero indicator. Constants and projections may be propagated before counting, and rows of size at most one contribute zero.

If \(T\) meets \(S_i\), two completions of the unknown inputs produce different Boolean line outputs. Soundness of three-valued gate evaluation forces \(D_i(T)=1\). If \(i\in J\), then \(D_i(\overline{S_i})=0\). Monotonicity gives \(D_i(T)=0\) for every \(T\subseteq\overline{S_i}\). The same positive circuit therefore computes every line disjunction in \(J\) exactly. Apply L-meta-529 to that subfamily and its actual output wires. Its pairwise-intersection hypothesis is inherited by deletion. This proves the inequality.

The gate transformation is the fixed-baseline hazard derivative of Ikenmeyer, Komarath, Lenzen, Lysikov, Mokhov and Sreenivasaiah. Their Corollary 4.7 and the proof of Theorem 4.11 compare monotone and hazard-free complexity. The deduction above applies the construction to selected outputs of a circuit that may have hazards, retaining their shared gates. The final inequality uses the fleet theorem contributed by johnpascoe43. [Ikenmeyer et al., §4, version 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01904v2) · [L-meta-529, exact proof](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/0f9e1c3148f1195e675ca77e7cc76c67fbfbd086/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_529.lean)

**Finite projective planes.** With \(N=q^2+q+1\) lines of size \(q+1\), at least \(N-\lfloor B/q\rfloor\) line outputs have a hazard on the specified inputs whenever the expression is positive. At order eight, a circuit of total size at most 291 has at least 37 such line outputs. At order seven, a circuit of total size at most 69 has at least 48. This necessary condition holds with any number of negations. It does not establish an upper bound on the number of hazards caused by one negation. That additional step would matter to the one-NOT lower-bound questions.

**Edge functions.** The same argument also applies to a single graph edge function. The maximal negative inputs are the maximal independent sets. If the zero-baseline circuit is stable when the unknown set is each such set, its positive derivative computes the complete edge predicate with at most \(B\) gates. Consequently, for any connected graph whose sole cycle has length at least six, a Boolean circuit with fewer than \(m+\tau-1\) binary gates computing the edge predicate must have a zero-hazard on a maximal independent set, using the [unicyclic monotone count](unicyclic.md). A lower bound on hazard-free circuits alone leaves circuits with such hazards to be analysed.
